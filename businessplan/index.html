Your Business Plan

Your 1-Hour Business Plan

Write quickly. Don't overthink. You can refine later — right now, the goal is clarity on paper.

1.

Think about a real problem, difficulty or unmet need that people experience. What happens because of this problem, and why would someone want it solved?

2.

Be specific about the people or organisations you want to serve. Think about their circumstances, needs and what makes them likely to pay for a solution. Avoid broad answers like "everyone" or "small businesses".

3.

Describe what you could offer and how customers would receive it. Would it be a product, service or something else? Explain what the customer would get and how your solution would address their problem.

4.

Consider your experience, skills, knowledge or understanding of the problem. What gives you an advantage or makes you credible? Be honest about any gaps you may need to address.

5.

If you're already trading, consider your current prices. If you're starting out, suggest a realistic price. Think about your costs, what customers receive and what they might be willing to pay. It's fine to identify assumptions you still need to test.

6.

Think about specific ways to reach people who need what you offer. Consider existing contacts, referrals, communities, partnerships or marketing channels. What practical actions could you take to reach those five customers?

7.

Set a few realistic goals for the next three months. These might include testing demand, speaking to potential customers, making sales or improving something that isn't working. Be specific about what you want to achieve and how you'll know you've achieved it.
